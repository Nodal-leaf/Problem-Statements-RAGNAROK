Tolan Harbour handles smaller rice cargoes as Vanir ban holds

[P1] Rice cargoes arriving at Tolan Harbour are smaller and less frequent than a year ago, port agents said.

[P2] Vanir's ordinary white rice is no longer loading for Tolan, and the first extra cargoes from Valhalla are only now arriving.

[P3] Anchor Marine, which provides port services at Tolan Harbour, said berths were open and there was no congestion.

[P4] Tara Quill, a customs officer, said declarations for premium rice and contract cargoes were being processed separately.

[P5] The port has not changed its fees for rice cargoes, though agents said smaller lots cost more per tonne to handle.

[P6] Port agents said the average size of a rice cargo had fallen, and that some ships were leaving with unused space.

[P7] The harbour has kept its normal schedule, but the number of rice sailings is lower than a year ago.

[P8] Anchor Marine said it had redeployed some staff to handle the extra paperwork for premium and contract cargoes.

[P9] Customs officers are checking each declaration against the exemption rules, which has slowed clearance for some shipments.

[P10] Buyers said the smaller cargoes were more expensive per tonne, even though the port's headline fees had not changed.

[P11] Traders expect the arrival pattern to stay uneven until Valhalla's extra crop is ready.

[P12] Buyers expect arrivals to stay below normal until Valhalla's extra crop reaches the market.
