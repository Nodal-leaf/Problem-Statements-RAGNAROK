Valhalla rice exports rise but cannot fill Vanir gap

[P1] Valhalla's rice exports have risen since February, but traders say the increase covers only part of the volume lost to Vanir's ban.

[P2] The country can raise shipments by about 30 to 35 percent on its small base, according to the Valhalla trade ministry.

[P3] Extra cargoes are moving to Tolan and Midgard, where buyers are paying about 20 to 25 percent more than a year ago.

[P4] Marcus Venn, a commodity trader, said Valhalla was reluctant to sell too much while domestic prices were rising.

[P5] Ingrid Stahl, a trade official, repeated that Valhalla has no plan to restrict exports.

[P6] Valhalla's farmers have planted more rice, but the extra crop will not be ready until later in the year.

[P7] The trade ministry said the country would honour its existing contracts and would not introduce a ban of its own.

[P8] Traders said Valhalla was selling more to Tolan and Midgard, and less to buyers who were willing to pay a lower price.

[P9] The price of Valhalla rice has risen in line with the open-market price, which has made the extra volume more valuable.

[P10] A buyer in Tolan said Valhalla's shipments were welcome but arrived too late to prevent a sharp fall in stocks.

[P11] The ministry said it would review the export position if domestic prices rose further.

[P12] Analysts expect the gain to fade once Vanir's quota begins, but that is outside the current quarter.

[P13] Port agents in Valhalla said the extra cargoes were leaving from the usual terminals without delay.

[P14] The first larger shipments arrived two months after the Vanir ban, in line with the capacity limit.
